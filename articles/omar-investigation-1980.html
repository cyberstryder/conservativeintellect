Investigation of Ilhan Omar Began in 1980, Two Years Before She Was Born, Columnist Announces; the 1980, Records Show, Is the First Year on the Warrant's Date Window, and the Unsealed Documents Contain No Charges

By Chip Hollister November 24, 2026 4 min read
📜

WASHINGTON — The investigation of Rep. Ilhan Omar began in 1980, a columnist announced Monday, in a breakthrough for prosecutors everywhere, who can now open cases against suspects up to two years before the suspects exist.

“The investigation actually started in 1980. Omar wasn't even born yet,” wrote Matt Margolis of PJ Media, reviewing the search-warrant records unsealed Monday from the October 12 search of Omar's Washington home. “That's even more proof politics has nothing to do with this.” He added that the documents “clearly show that the case against Omar is legitimate.”

The 1980 in the records is doing a different job. It is the opening year of the date window on the warrant — prosecutors sought evidence of violations “occurring between 1980 to the present,” a standard, maximally wide window of the kind warrants use so that nothing relevant falls outside it. A window is not a start date. No investigator opened a file in 1980 on a child who, on the birth date prosecutors themselves are examining, arrived on October 4, 1982, in Mogadishu, spent four years in a Kenyan refugee camp, and reached the United States in 1995.

“We date our investigations from the first year printed on the form,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who reads court filings the way other men read box scores. “By that standard I've been under investigation by the phone book since 1954. Devastating documents, those. Mostly coupons.”

What the unsealed documents actually show is narrower, and it is printed in the same reporting Margolis was summarizing. The filings contained, in the New York Post's words, “no potential charges.” Prosecutors described themselves as “in the throes of a preindictment investigation.” Every reference to the specific laws allegedly broken is redacted. The 67-page affidavit laying out the government's evidence remains sealed, by order of Chief Judge James Boasberg, who kept the “vast majority” of the records under seal. And the suspected conduct, per people familiar with the probe, concerns possible immigration fraud “committed by her or family members” who fled Somalia in the 1990s — which is how a filing can reach back toward 1980 without alleging that a not-yet-born Omar committed anything in it.

None of that makes the investigation trivial, and this newspaper takes no position on what the sealed affidavit will eventually show. It merely observes the distance between the record and the summary. The record says: a wide date window, redacted offenses, no charges, a sealed case. The summary says: an investigation older than its subject, proof of purity, and a legitimate case, clearly shown — three conclusions, each one read off a part of the document that is either boilerplate or blacked out.

At press time, the year 1980 had been returned to its window, where it continues to be the earliest date prosecutors were allowed to look at rather than the day anyone started looking, and the devastation had been located in the affidavit, which remains sealed, where it can be neither confirmed nor, conveniently, checked.

Actually True

Omar was born in 1982, after the warrant window opens. Encyclopaedia Britannica: Ilhan Omar was born October 4, 1982, in Mogadishu, Somalia; her family fled the Somali civil war, spent four years in a Kenyan refugee camp, was granted asylum and settled in the U.S. in 1995; she became a U.S. citizen at 17 in 2000. A 1980-to-present evidence window therefore begins before her birth and cannot, by itself, date the start of any investigation of her.

The 1980 date is a warrant date range, not an investigation start date. The unsealed filings (released Nov. 23, 2026, by Chief Judge James Boasberg, D.D.C.) sought evidence of violations “occurring between 1980 to the present.” Prosecutors wrote that they believe Omar committed redacted crimes “over decades — beginning long before she was ever in Congress.” Neither statement says an investigation began in 1980.

The unsealed documents contain no charges. The New York Post's report on the unsealing: the disclosures included an abbreviated inventory of items taken in the Oct. 12 search “but no potential charges”; prosecutors said they were “in the throes of a preindictment investigation”; all references to the specific statutes are redacted, and the 67-page supporting affidavit remains sealed.

The probe, per the same reporting, is about her or her family. Prosecutors in the D.C. and Minnesota U.S. Attorney's offices are building a case related to “possible immigration fraud committed by her or family members who fled war-torn Somalia in the 1990s,” including whether a birth date was misrepresented on government papers. Omar has denied wrongdoing and, asked Oct. 21 whether she married her brother, answered: “Not to my knowledge.”

What is true in the original post: the records were unsealed, they are serious, the government's theory reaches back decades, and a federal judge found enough to sign the warrant (Oct. 9, after revising the search protocol). That is a real story. “The investigation started in 1980, so politics has nothing to do with it and the case is clearly legitimate” is not in it.
