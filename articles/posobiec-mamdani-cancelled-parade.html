Parade Restraint

Mayor Cancels Macy's Parade for First Time in American History, Commentator Announces; the Parade, Records Show, Steps Off Saturday, Was Cancelled Three Times in the 1940s, and Was Moved by the Store That Owns It

By Chip Hollister November 26, 2026 4 min read
🎈

NEW YORK — The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was cancelled on Thursday by Mayor Zohran Mamdani, for the first time in American history, as an act of cultural vandalism. This was reported on X by Jack Posobiec, and it has the advantage over the competing accounts — Reuters, the Associated Press, CNN, the Wall Street Journal — of being much more dramatic, and the disadvantage of being wrong in every particular that can be checked.

"Of course Mamdani cancelled the Macy's Day Parade for the first time in American history," Posobiec wrote. "The cultural vandalism is the point."

Begin with the cancellation, which did not happen. The parade was postponed — moved, by Macy's, from Thursday morning to Saturday at 8:30 a.m., balloons, marching bands, and Santa intact. NBC, which broadcasts it, aired a rerun of last year's parade on Thursday to fill the hole. A cancelled parade does not ordinarily get a new date, a start time, and a rain-check broadcast, but this one did, because it is not cancelled. It is, at time of writing, roughly thirty-six hours away.

Continue with the mayor, who did not do it. The decision was Macy's — the retailer owns the parade — made "after close consultation with our City and agency partners" and announced Wednesday at a press conference where Macy's executive Will Coss stood next to Mamdani and Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch and explained it. If this was cultural vandalism, it was vandalism committed by a department store, in public, a day in advance, with the police commissioner's assistance, for reasons the store published in a statement. The mayor's role in the plot is further complicated by the fact that he will not attend Saturday's parade either: he is having scheduled surgery on a ruptured Achilles tendon he tore playing soccer. The vandal has an alibi, and it is orthopedic.

Finish with the history, which is the part the post was most confident about. A cancellation would not have been the first in American history, because the parade was cancelled in 1942, and again in 1943, and again in 1944 — suspended for World War II, when the rubber in the balloons was needed elsewhere. What is a first, and it is a genuinely interesting one, is a postponement: in a hundred years the parade had been rained on, snowed on, and once, in 1971, stripped of its balloons by wind, but it had never before been moved to another day. The record book had a new entry available. It just wasn't the one in the post.

The reason for the move is documented to a degree that leaves little room for vandalism. A nor'easter hit the East Coast overnight, knocking out power to more than 250,000 customers by Thursday morning. The National Weather Service had a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning up for New York City, with sustained winds of 30 to 40 miles per hour and gusts forecast as high as 60 to 65. City rules — written after the 1997 parade, when a gust tore the Cat in the Hat into a lamppost and put a spectator in a coma — bar the giant balloons from flying above 23 miles per hour sustained or 34 in gusts. The forecast cleared both limits by a factor of two. The balloons were deflated Wednesday night and trucked to New Jersey, where they will be re-inflated for Saturday — a second inflation the New York Post priced at roughly $1.7 million in scarce helium, a figure Posobiec himself relayed seventeen minutes after the cancellation post, in a follow-up that described the parade, accurately this time, as postponed.

"You cannot cancel a parade that is happening on Saturday," said Marla Dench, 61, a fictional retired parade-route marshal who has held a balloon line in three decades of Thanksgivings. "You can move it. We moved it. The balloons are in New Jersey, not in a gulag. I have held Spider-Man in a forty-mile gust and I have held him in a calm, and I am telling you the gust does not care who the mayor is."

There is a real story here, and this site told a version of it this morning when another influencer attributed the same postponement to imaginary riots: a century-old tradition moved for the first time in its history because a storm outranked it, under a safety rule written in a spectator's blood in 1997. That story has a mayor in it, briefly, standing at a podium saying safety comes first before going off to have his foot repaired. Turning it into a first-in-history cancellation requires deleting Saturday, deleting 1942 through 1944, deleting Macy's, and deleting the weather — four deletions, each one checkable in about a minute, in a post that has been up all afternoon.

At press time, the parade remained scheduled for Saturday, the storm was moving off the coast on the forecast's schedule rather than the mayor's, and the balloons were resting in New Jersey — postponed, like the truth in the original post, but expected to arrive within forty-eight hours.

Actually True

The parade was postponed, not cancelled. Macy's announced on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, that the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade would move from Thursday, November 26, to Saturday, November 28, at 8:30 a.m. ET, because of a forecast nor'easter. NBC aired a rerun of the 2025 parade in the Thursday slot (Reuters, CNN, AP).

It was Macy's decision, announced in public. Macy's said it acted "after close consultation with our City and agency partners"; its executive Will Coss announced the move Wednesday at a press conference with Mayor Zohran Mamdani and NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch (USA Today, WSJ, People).

A cancellation would not have been a first. The parade was suspended from 1942 to 1944 during World War II. What is unprecedented is a weather postponement — the first rescheduling in the event's history; its balloons had previously been grounded once, in 1971 (CNN, AP, Reuters).

The weather is documented, not alleged. The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for New York City for Thursday into Friday; Mamdani cited sustained winds of 30–40 mph with gusts of 60–65 mph, and the storm knocked out power to more than 250,000 customers from New Jersey to North Carolina by Thursday morning (WSJ, Reuters).

The balloons are legally grounded well below the forecast. City rules bar the giant balloons from flying when sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts exceed 34 mph — rules adopted after the 1997 parade, when a wind-blown Cat in the Hat balloon struck a lamppost and put a spectator in a coma (WSJ, AP, NY Post).

The mayor won't be at the rescheduled parade either. Mamdani said Wednesday he will miss Saturday's parade because of scheduled surgery to repair a ruptured Achilles tendon suffered in a soccer match (CNN, USA Today).

Posobiec's own feed corrected the verb. Seventeen minutes after the "cancelled" post, he shared the New York Post's report that Macy's faces a roughly $1.7 million helium bill to inflate the balloons a second time, describing the event in his own text as one Mamdani "postpones" (NY Post, Nov. 26, 2026; X API read of @JackPosobiec, Nov. 26, 2026).

What is true in the original post: the parade did not step off on Thanksgiving for the first time in its hundred years, and the mayor supported the decision. Everything else — cancelled, first cancellation in American history, the mayor as author, cultural vandalism as motive — is unsupported or contradicted by the record.
