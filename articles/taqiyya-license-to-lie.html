Iran's Religion Lets It Lie to Infidels Anytime It Wants, Internet Explains; the Doctrine, Scholars Note, Is About Not Getting Killed for Your Faith

By Chip Hollister November 22, 2026 4 min read
☽

Watch Chip Hollister tell it:

Tehran — Diplomacy with Iran is pointless, the internet explained Sunday, because Iran’s religion gives its negotiators a standing permission slip to lie to infidels anytime they want — a doctrine that, on inspection, turns out to be about concealing your faith when someone is trying to kill you for it, which is a different permission slip.

“Doesn’t matter what deals they make,” wrote one foreign-policy analyst on Reddit’s r/Conservative, in a thread about President Trump’s push for a permanent deal guaranteeing Tehran never gets a nuclear weapon. “Their religion allows them to lie to Infidels anytime they want.”

The doctrine in question is taqiyya, a word that has done more work in comment sections than in any seminary. In the actual sources, taqiyya means concealing one’s belief and skipping ordinary religious duties when under threat of death or injury — self-protection, derived from a word meaning “to shield oneself.” It is associated mainly with Shia Muslims, who developed it while being persecuted, at various times, by non-Muslims and by the Sunni majority alike. Iran is Shia-majority, which makes it the one country where the comment almost lands, in the same way a stopped clock almost tells the time.

“A license to lie at a negotiating table would be extremely convenient. But hiding your religion so a mob doesn’t murder you is not a negotiating tactic. That’s just hiding.”

“A license to lie at a negotiating table would be extremely convenient,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who negotiates exclusively at flea markets. “But hiding your religion so a mob doesn’t murder you is not a negotiating tactic. That’s just hiding. I do the same thing with my opinions about my in-laws, and nobody calls it a doctrine.”

The Qur’anic verses behind the doctrine are 16:106 — covering a believer compelled to renounce his faith while his heart stays secure in it — and 3:28, about taking precaution against those who would harm you. Classical commentators quoted by the Yaqeen Institute put the threshold plainly: taqiyya “is not permissible except with fear of death or loss of limb or tremendous suffering.” Anytime-you-want, scholars noted, is doing a great deal of lifting in the original claim, most of it overhead.

Negotiators reached for comment said they planned to keep verifying centrifuge counts the old-fashioned way, with inspectors, satellites, and seals on equipment, rather than by asking whether the other side’s religion permits fibbing — a verification regime experts described as “how arms control has always worked, because nobody signs a treaty on the honor system with anybody, of any faith, ever.”

At press time, the thread had moved on to its next finding, a definition of insanity confidently attributed to Albert Einstein, who never said it — a reminder that the only tradition with a documented, anytime-it-wants approach to quotation is the internet itself.

Actually True

Taqiyya is concealment under mortal threat, not a general licence to deceive. Britannica defines taqiyyah as “concealing one’s belief and foregoing ordinary religious duties when under threat of death or injury,” derived from waqa (“to shield oneself”) — self-protection in the face of danger, employed mainly by the Shi’ah because of their historical persecution by both non-Muslims and the Sunni majority (Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Taqiyyah”).

The scholarly threshold is death, limb, or tremendous suffering — not convenience. The Yaqeen Institute’s “Playing the Taqiyya Card” (Imam Omar Suleiman, 2017) traces the doctrine to Qur’an 16:106 and 3:28 and quotes the classical commentary (Tafsir al-Qurtubi): “Taqiyya is not permissible except with fear of death or loss of limb or tremendous suffering.” The paper calls the claim that Islam teaches Muslims to lie to non-Muslims a “grotesque misrepresentation” of a ruling meant for believers saving themselves from mortal danger.

Encyclopaedia Iranica agrees, in almost the same words. Its TAQIYA entry defines the practice as “hiding beliefs or rituals in times of imminent harm to one, his/her property, or reputation” — a survival practice “commonly regarded as distinctively Shi’ite,” not a negotiating privilege (Encyclopaedia Iranica, “TAQIYA,” Louis Medoff).

The context was a real, current negotiation. President Trump told Fox News on Saturday, November 21, 2026, that he is seeking a permanent deal that will ensure Iran does not acquire a nuclear weapon — “I’m going to make a deal that is going to be permanent” — while Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi maintained that keeping enriched uranium inside Iran remains a “red line” (JNS, Nov. 21, 2026; The Media Line, Nov. 21, 2026). Whatever one thinks of Tehran’s credibility, arms deals are enforced by inspection and verification regimes, not by theology.

Sources: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Taqiyyah”; Yaqeen Institute, “Playing the Taqiyya Card” (2017); Encyclopaedia Iranica, “TAQIYA”; JNS (Nov. 21, 2026) and The Media Line (Nov. 21, 2026) on the Trump permanent-deal remarks.
