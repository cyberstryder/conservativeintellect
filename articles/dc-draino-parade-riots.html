Parade Restraint

Parade Postponed to Hide Riots Nobody Has Seen, Influencer Bets; the Storm, Records Show, Filed a High Wind Warning, Gusts to 65, and Held a Press Conference

By Biff Carrington November 26, 2026 4 min read
🎈

NEW YORK — The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed Thursday for the first time in its hundred years, and within hours an influencer had worked out why: not the nor'easter the National Weather Service had been warning about all week, but "leftist riots" the mayor did not want "seen by the entire country."

"Mamdani says he's postponing NYC's Thanksgiving day parade for the 1st time in over 100 years due to 'weather'," wrote DC_Draino, placing the weather in quotation marks, where weather goes when it is lying. "How much you want to bet it's actually because of these leftist riots that he doesn't want seen by the entire country?"

Begin, as always, with the part that is true, because here the true part is unusually load-bearing. The parade really was postponed. Macy's announced Wednesday that its 100th parade would move from Thanksgiving morning to Saturday at 8:30 a.m., the first weather rescheduling in the event's history — the parade was cancelled outright from 1942 to 1944 for the war, and its balloons were grounded once, in 1971, but it had never before been pushed to another day. The reason given was a storm, and the storm, inconveniently for the theory, is documented in the way storms are: the National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for New York City running Thursday into Friday, forecast sustained winds of 30 to 40 miles per hour with gusts Mamdani put at 60 to 65, and expected one to three inches of rain on top.

The balloons have their own law, and it is not new. Since 1997 — when 43-mile-per-hour gusts tore the Cat in the Hat loose, slammed it into a lamppost, and left a woman in a coma — city rules prohibit the giant balloons from flying when sustained winds exceed 23 miles per hour or gusts exceed 34. Thursday's forecast cleared both bars before breakfast. The decision, such as it was, had been made by arithmetic sometime around the moment the forecast printed.

Nor was the decision Mamdani's to hide behind. It was Macy's. "The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration," said the retailer, whose executive Will Coss announced it Wednesday at an outdoor press conference alongside the mayor and Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch. The balloons, already inflated for the traditional Wednesday blow-up, were deflated that night and trucked to New Jersey to wait out the storm. This is the cover-up: announced a day early, on camera, by the parade's owner, next to the police commissioner, with the evidence — a hundred deflated balloons — driven across the Hudson in trucks.

Of the riots, the record is thinner. Reuters covered the postponement. So did the Associated Press, CNN, USA Today, the Wall Street Journal, and the New York Post, a paper not historically shy about leftist disorder in New York City. None of them mentions a riot, a protest, or a single arrest in connection with the parade decision, because the coverage is about wind speeds, balloon regulations, and a mayor explaining that he will miss Saturday's rescheduled parade anyway, owing to a scheduled surgery on a ruptured Achilles tendon he picked up playing soccer. A mayor staging a weather hoax to conceal riots from the entire country might be expected to at least attend the event he rescheduled. This one is going to the hospital.

"You can't fly a seventy-foot Garfield in a sixty-mile-an-hour gust, and I say that as a man who has tried," said Hank Pruitt, 58, a fictional former balloon handler who speaks of the 1997 parade the way sailors speak of a particular wave. "People think the balloons are the show. The balloons are a liability waiver with a face painted on it. The wind doesn't care about your politics. It barely cares about the balloon."

There is, in the end, a real story available here, and it is even a good one: a hundred-year-old tradition moved for the first time in its history, a storm strong enough to ground Spider-Man, a city's quarter-century-old safety rule doing exactly what it was written to do. It requires no riots. The quotation marks around "weather" are doing all the work in Thursday's post, and they are carrying a theory that the forecast, the warning, the rulebook, the press conference, and the deflated balloons all decline to support. The weather was real. The riots are the part in quotation marks.

At press time, the storm was arriving on schedule over New York City, indifferent to coverage, and the balloons were resting in New Jersey, where they are expected to remain until Saturday — hidden, presumably, from the entire country, in a warehouse, by the wind.

Actually True

The postponement is real, and it is a first. Macy's announced on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, that the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade would move from Thursday, November 26, to Saturday, November 28, at 8:30 a.m. ET, due to a forecast nor'easter — the first time in the parade's history it has been rescheduled for weather (Reuters, CNN, USA Today, AP). The parade was cancelled in 1942–1944 for World War II and its balloons were grounded once, in 1971, but it had never been postponed to another day.

The weather is documented, not alleged. The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for New York City for Thursday into Friday. Forecasts called for sustained winds of 25–40 mph with gusts of 50–65 mph and 1–3 inches of rain; the city banned empty tractor-trailers from bridges Thursday morning because of the wind (Reuters, WSJ, weather.com).

The balloons are legally grounded well below the forecast. City rules bar the giant balloons from flying when sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts exceed 34 mph — rules adopted after the 1997 parade, when a wind-blown Cat in the Hat balloon struck a lamppost and put a spectator in a coma (AP, NY Post, weather.com).

It was Macy's decision, announced in public. Macy's said it moved the parade "after close consultation with our City and agency partners," and its executive Will Coss announced the change Wednesday at a press conference with Mayor Zohran Mamdani and NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch. The inflated balloons were deflated Wednesday night and moved to a New Jersey facility for Saturday (USA Today, WSJ, NY Post).

No source connects the decision to riots or protests. Coverage by Reuters, AP, CNN, USA Today, the Wall Street Journal, and the New York Post attributes the postponement solely to the storm and balloon safety; none mentions riots, demonstrations, or public disorder as a factor. The post offers no evidence for the riot claim beyond the attached link.

The mayor won't even be at the rescheduled parade. Mamdani said Wednesday he will miss Saturday's parade because of scheduled surgery to repair a ruptured Achilles tendon suffered in a soccer match (CNN, USA Today).

What is true in the original post: the parade was postponed, for the first time in its roughly 100-year history, and weather was the stated reason. The scare quotes and the riots are the false parts, and they are the parts that make the post a story.
