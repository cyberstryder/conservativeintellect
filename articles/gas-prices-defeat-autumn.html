Gas Prices Fall for Eighth Straight Week; Congresswoman Takes Credit, Defeats Autumn

By Chip Hollister November 18, 2026 3 min read
⛽

Washington — Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene announced Wednesday that the price of gasoline dropped after the midterm elections “because you got whooped in the midterms BECAUSE YOU have been psychotically threatening to basically nuke Iran,” adding, “So the market reacted.”

The announcement surprised the nation’s refineries, which had been under the impression they were switching to cheaper winter-blend gasoline, as they do every fall, and motorists, who had been watching prices fall since September.

“I filled up in October and it was already cheaper, but I guess that was just a preview,” said Gary Pruitt, 61, a local motorist and self-described “gas price historian.” “Turns out the real price drop was locked in a vault until the election was over. Incredible discipline by the gasoline.”

“Turns out the real price drop was locked in a vault until the election was over. Incredible discipline by the gasoline.”

Energy analysts at the Institute for Seasonal Studies confirmed that autumn has now been defeated and will be replaced, going forward, with a second summer, pending a recount.

“The market reacted,” Greene explained, in what economists are calling the most complete theory of energy pricing ever posted. At press time, the market had also reacted to winter approaching, demand falling, and refineries running — three junior-varsity reactions that experts say were nowhere near as decisive as the whooping.

Actually True

Greene’s post gets the timeline backwards. The national average peaked at $4.478 on September 21 (EIA) and had already fallen to $4.153 by November 2 — before a single vote was counted. AAA put the average at $3.98 on November 10, the first reading under $4 since July.

GasBuddy’s Patrick De Haan calls the election theory “a myth”: pump prices have fallen in the 30 days before the election in nearly every year since 2006, because refiners switch to cheaper winter-blend fuel (allowed from September 1 this year), finish maintenance (refineries ran at 94.5% of capacity in late October), and demand fades with the weather.

Crude oil did fall after the election — but on bets the Iran war would end, not because of threats to escalate it. Brent dropped 7.6% on November 2 after Iran signaled it would keep talking and President Trump twice said “No” when asked if he would strike Iran; it fell again after Democrats won both chambers, and again on November 9 when Trump said Iran was “militarily effectively over.”
