80% of Americans Support ICE, Kari Lake Announces; the Poll She Is Describing Found 80% of Republicans, 40% of Americans, and a 60% Majority Saying the Opposite

By Biff Carrington November 24, 2026 4 min read
📊

PHOENIX — Eighty percent of Americans support the work ICE is doing, Kari Lake announced Tuesday, in a finding that required reading exactly one column of a polling table and promoting it, without paperwork, to the entire country.

“We're seeing coordinated efforts by radicals to push their open-borders agenda by smearing the men & women of @ICEgov,” Lake wrote. “80% of Americans support the work ICE is doing to remove illegal immigrants. A small group of paid radicals is flooding the internet with clips to make it look” — the post continued, though the arithmetic had already left.

The number 80 does appear in the polling. It appears twice, in fact, and neither time does it mean what the post says. In the Marquette Law School national poll — 1,003 adults, conducted January 21–28, 2026 — approval of how ICE is handling its job stands at 40%, with 60% disapproving, unchanged from the same poll the November before. Broken out by party, however, 80% of Republicans approve. The other columns read 23% of independents and 5% of Democrats, which is how a table with an 80 in it produces a country at 40.

The second 80 is narrower still. Marquette's poll director, Charles Franklin, noted that when pollsters ask about deporting violent criminals, support runs over 80% — and then falls to 44% for people who have lived here a long time with no criminal record. Deporting violent criminals is a policy nearly everyone endorses. “The work ICE is doing” is a different question, and the country has been answering it, at length, all year.

“Eighty percent is a real number,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who reads polling tables exclusively through campaign posts. “It's just the number for the first column. If you read the whole table, you get forty, and forty is less inspiring, so I understand the editing decision. I just wouldn't call it a summary. I'd call it a promotion. The column got promoted to country.”

The rest of the year's polling declines to rescue the figure. Pew Research Center, surveying 5,062 adults from September 28 to October 4, 2026, found ICE at 35% favorable and 56% unfavorable — the least popular of the twelve federal agencies tested, below the IRS. Even Republicans in Pew's survey came in at 67% favorable, meaning the 80 could not be located among Republicans anymore either by the fall. The NBC News exit poll of the November 3 midterms found voters at 41% approve, 59% disapprove. Change Research, in January, found 48% supporting ICE's deportation raids and 51% opposed. And the White House's own February roundup of the friendliest polls it could find topped out at 61% for deporting illegal aliens generally and 54% for ICE enforcing immigration laws — the administration's cherry orchard, picked clean, still 19 points short.

None of this makes the underlying politics simple, and this newspaper takes no position on whether ICE should be cheered or booed. It merely observes that “80% of Americans” is a claim about 340 million people, and the actual Americans, asked repeatedly by Marquette, Pew, NBC, and Change Research across ten months, have answered 40, 35, 41, and 43-to-48 — a consensus, of a kind, just not the kind that fits in a post about a small group of paid radicals inventing an unpopular impression. The impression, it turns out, was measured. The 80 was not.

At press time, the small group of paid radicals had been located and identified as a majority of respondents in four national surveys, and the 80% had been returned to its column, where it continues to describe Republicans, who were always welcome to it.

Actually True

  • National approval of ICE's work is 40%, not 80%. The Marquette Law School Poll (national survey, Jan. 21–28, 2026; 1,003 adults; margin of error ±3.4 points) found 40% of adults approve of the way ICE is handling its job and 60% disapprove — unchanged from Marquette's November 2025 reading of the same question.
  • The 80% figure is the Republican column of that same poll. Marquette found 80% of Republicans approve of ICE's handling of its job, versus 23% of independents and 5% of Democrats. Lake's post takes the partisan subgroup's number and attributes it to “Americans.”
  • The other real ~80% is about violent criminals only. Marquette poll director Charles Franklin: “When we ask about deporting violent criminals, over 80% are in favor of that, but when we ask about people who've been here for a long time and have no criminal record, that support drops to 44%.” Broad support for one narrow policy is not support for “the work ICE is doing.”
  • Every other major 2026 survey agrees, including the recent ones. Pew Research Center (Sept. 28–Oct. 4, 2026; 5,062 adults): ICE 35% favorable / 56% unfavorable, the lowest-rated of 12 federal agencies, with even Republicans at 67% favorable. NBC News exit poll (Nov. 3, 2026 midterms): 41% of voters approve of ICE, 59% disapprove. Change Research (Jan. 13–19, 2026; 1,348 Americans): 48% support ICE deportation raids nationwide, 51% oppose; ICE rated 43% favorable / 53% unfavorable.
  • Even the White House's cherry-picked numbers don't reach 80%. The administration's February 2, 2026 roundup of favorable polls cited 61% supporting deportation of illegal aliens and 54% supporting ICE enforcing federal immigration laws — the highest general figures friendly polling produced all year.

Sources: Marquette Law School Poll, national survey Jan. 21–28, 2026 (press release and director Charles Franklin's remarks); Pew Research Center, “How Americans View Federal Agencies” (survey of 5,062 U.S. adults, Sept. 28–Oct. 4, 2026); NBC News Exit Poll, Nov. 3, 2026; Change Research, “Nation Divided on Immigration Enforcement” (Jan. 13–19, 2026); The White House, “Americans Overwhelmingly Support Deporting Criminal Illegals, Local Cooperation with ICE” (Feb. 2, 2026).
