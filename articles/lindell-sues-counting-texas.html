Pillow Magnate Sues Counting Itself After Texas Votes Go “Backwards” All the Way to a Typo

By Chip Hollister November 20, 2026 3 min read
⚖

Austin, Texas — Mike Lindell announced Thursday that he is preparing a class-action lawsuit against the voting machine companies, having uncovered “thousands of examples of vote counts going BACKWARDS and hundreds of cases where reported votes exceed the number of voters” in Texas, a state that responded to the news by continuing to have counted its votes in the normal direction.

“THEY STOLE TEXAS!” Lindell added, in a follow-up statement that election officials described as the most thoroughly investigated claim in the post, in that it contained a state.

The centerpiece evidence — a vote total that appeared to plunge on live television — turned out to be a television graphic in which a staffer typed 22,403 instead of 224,403, an error the network’s own analyst flagged on air and fixed within minutes. Investigators for the pillow magnate called the correction “the most suspicious part,” noting that real fraud would never correct itself that quickly, or at all, or be a typo.

“If somebody stuffed the ballot box, they stuffed it to a little over half full, which is a weird flex.”

Texas Governor Greg Abbott, in a development the lawsuit describes as “deeply compromised,” congratulated the Democratic winner of his own race, apparently fooled by the same arithmetic that produced a 51.6% turnout — a figure experts note is lower than the turnout in 2018, and considerably lower than the number of voters.

“We counted 9.85 million ballots from 19 million registered voters,” said one county election worker, who asked not to be named because she has to do this again in two years. “If somebody stuffed the ballot box, they stuffed it to a little over half full, which is a weird flex.”

At press time, the class action’s first named defendant was expected to be the number line, with the concept of subtraction listed as a co-conspirator. Donations are being accepted at the usual address.

Actually True

Democrats did sweep Texas’s statewide offices on November 3 — James Talarico beat Ken Paxton for the U.S. Senate (the first Democrat to win a Texas Senate seat since 1988) and Gina Hinojosa beat Greg Abbott for governor, 52.8% to 45.7% in unofficial returns. Abbott congratulated Hinojosa, and both losing Republicans conceded.

9.85 million Texans voted, out of roughly 19 million registered — a 51.6% turnout, below 2018’s 53%. There were not more votes than voters; there were barely votes from half the voters. The viral “20 million found votes” version of the claim is arithmetically impossible: it would more than double Texas’s registration, and exceed the total ballots cast by ten million.

The famous “backwards” count was a typo in an NBC on-screen graphic — a dropped digit in Paxton’s Tarrant County total (22,403 shown instead of 224,403) — which Steve Kornacki questioned on air and the network corrected within minutes, per a Lead Stories fact check.

All 254 Texas counties shifted toward Democrats versus 2022, with turnout surging in the border counties (Starr County went from 57% for Trump in 2024 to over 75% for Talarico). An unusual pattern — and the state’s risk-limiting audit is still underway and the official canvass hasn’t happened yet, which is the normal process for checking.

Lindell’s court record on the same family of claims: a federal jury ordered him to pay $2.3 million for defaming a former Dominion employee; in September 2026 a judge found he had defamed Smartmatic; and Dominion’s $1.3 billion suit against him ended in a confidential settlement.
