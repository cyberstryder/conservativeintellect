Every Child in America Will Receive Two SpaceX Shares, Billionaire Announces; His Own Company, in Its Own Announcement, Promised One Share to Every "Currently Active Trump Account" — a Citizens-Only Account Nobody Has Counted

By Chip Hollister November 26, 2026 4 min read
🚀

AUSTIN — Every child in America will receive two SpaceX shares, the company's chief executive announced early Thursday — one from the company, and one from him personally. It is a generous sentence. It is also a larger sentence than the one his company issued.

SpaceX's own announcement, released Wednesday, promises one newly issued share of Class A stock to every currently active Trump Account, with Mr. Musk matching the company's total share for share. A Trump Account is a federal investment account for children, and it comes with a guest list: the child must be a U.S. citizen, under 18, with a valid Social Security number. Children in America who are not citizens — and there are rather a lot of children in America — cannot have one at all. They will receive, under the company's actual announcement, the same number of SpaceX shares as the rest of us received before Wednesday, which is none.

Then there is the word "active," doing more work in this story than any rocket. The Treasury Department said in October that nearly 70 million Trump Accounts had been created, more than 60 million of them automatically, for children the government identified by Social Security number. But an automatically created account is an account-shaped object: a parent or guardian must claim it before it can be managed or take ordinary contributions, and as of Thursday morning neither SpaceX nor the Treasury had said what "currently active" means or how many accounts meet the definition. The company's release contains no number of shares, no cutoff date, and no definition. The $18 billion figure in the headlines is what the gift costs if roughly 70 million accounts qualify — an assumption printed as a total.

"We read the announcement the way we read all announcements: the big one first, then the company's," said Dale Krebbs, 54, a fictional local man whose own children have been promised, by his count, four shares, two bicycles, and a pony, in that order of reliability. "The big one says every child. The company's says every active account. My kids are children. Whether they are an active account is between them and the Treasury Department."

The calendar, too, is doing quiet editing. Of the two shares each qualifying child will eventually receive, one — the company's — transfers next month. The other, Mr. Musk's personal match, transfers when his lockup from SpaceX's June public offering expires, in June of next year: one share now, one share in seven months, which is not quite the arithmetic of "will receive two." And whichever share arrives cannot be spent on anything a child might want: gifted stock in a Trump Account generally cannot be sold for five years, and money in the accounts generally cannot come out until the year the child turns 18. A share given to a newborn this December is, functionally, a very small rocket that cannot launch until 2044.

None of this makes the underlying gift small, and this newspaper will not pretend otherwise. A newly issued share in every active account, matched personally by the chief executive, at Wednesday's closing price of $127.44, is real money on a national scale, and it follows an October pledge by SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell and her husband covering more than two million children — a number that, notably, was a number. The distance being measured here is not between generosity and stinginess. It is between "every currently active Trump Account," which is what was promised in writing, and "every child in America," which is what was posted. One of those phrases has eligibility rules, a claiming step, an unpublished count, and a seven-month wait for the second half. The other fits in a post.

At press time, every child in America had been located, several million of them ineligible for the account the shares are actually going into, the word "active" had been asked for its papers and had produced none, and the second share was en route at the speed of a lockup agreement, expected June 2027, weather permitting.

Actually True

SpaceX's announcement is narrower than Musk's post. SpaceX said Wednesday it would contribute one newly issued Class A share to every currently active Trump Account, with Musk personally matching the company's number of shares (Reuters, Nov. 25–26, 2026). Musk's post says "every child in America."

Not every child in America can have a Trump Account. Eligibility requires the child to be a U.S. citizen, under 18, with a valid Social Security number (IRS guidance; CNN Trump Accounts FAQ). Non-citizen children living in America are excluded entirely.

"Currently active" is undefined and uncounted. Nearly 70 million accounts had been created by October, 60 million-plus by automatic enrollment (Treasury, via FOX Business and CNN), but auto-created accounts must be claimed by a parent or guardian to be managed or receive ordinary contributions, and neither SpaceX nor the Treasury has defined "active" or published an active count. SpaceX's release gives no share total and no cutoff date.

The two shares arrive seven months apart. Per Musk's own follow-up post, the company share transfers in December 2026; his personal matching share transfers when his IPO lockup expires in June 2027.

The shares are locked up too. Gifted stock in a Trump Account generally cannot be sold for five years (or until the year the child turns 17, if sooner), and account funds generally cannot be withdrawn before the year the child turns 18 (Treasury rules, via CNBC/CNN).

What is true in the original post: the donation is real, it is one share from SpaceX plus a personal match from Musk, and at Wednesday's $127.44 close it would be worth roughly $18 billion if about 70 million accounts qualify. "Every child in America" is the part the fine print doesn't support.
