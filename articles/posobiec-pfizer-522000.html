Pfizer Shots "Linked to 331,500–522,000 American Deaths," Commentator Announces; the Study, Records Show, Compared Pfizer to Moderna, Attributed No Deaths to Either Vaccine, and Never Estimated Any Such Thing

By Biff Carrington November 25, 2026 5 min read
💉

WASHINGTON — A government study has found a catastrophic loss of life, a commentator announced Wednesday, estimating that Pfizer's mRNA shots alone "may have been linked to 331,500–522,000 American deaths." The study, reached for comment in the journal where it was actually published, reports that it estimated no such thing.

"🚨 URGENT: A government study reportedly found a catastrophic loss of life associated with COVID 💉," wrote Jack Posobiec. "Examining data from an entire state, the study estimates that Pfizer's mRNA shots alone may have been linked to 331,500–522,000 American deaths. Nicolas Hulscher explains."

Begin with the study, because there is one, and it deserves to be met on its own terms. On November 4, researchers led by MIT's Retsef Levi and Florida Surgeon General Joseph Ladapo — most of the team from the Florida Department of Health, working from state records — published a matched-cohort analysis in Global Epidemiology. From about 11.3 million eligible Florida adults who got a first COVID shot between December 2020 and August 2021, they paired 981,411 Pfizer recipients with 981,411 Moderna recipients, matched on age group, sex, race, ethnicity, month of vaccination, type of vaccination site, and census tract, and counted deaths over the next twelve months. They found 7,690 deaths in the Pfizer group and 5,519 in the Moderna group — a hazard ratio of 1.4, the source of the "40% higher" figure. That is the finding. It is a comparison of one mRNA vaccine against another mRNA vaccine. There is no third group. There is no unvaccinated baseline. And there is, anywhere in the paper, no number resembling 331,500 or 522,000, because the paper studied Florida and estimated nothing about America.

Those numbers belong to Nicolas Hulscher, the explainer Wednesday's post sends readers to, and they are arithmetic, not findings. The smaller one multiplies the study's observed gap — 221 more deaths per 100,000 Pfizer recipients than Moderna recipients — by an assumed 150 million American Pfizer recipients: 331,500. Note what that calculation actually says even if you grant every assumption inside it: 331,500 additional deaths compared with having taken Moderna instead. It is a difference between two vaccines, re-labelled as a body count for one of them. The larger number abandons the study's data altogether: it takes the 40% relative hazard, applies it to 150 million people, and multiplies by an assumed annual mortality of 0.87% — a baseline Hulscher substituted for the study's own measured Moderna mortality rate of 0.562%. Swap the study's real baseline back in and the headline figure shrinks by roughly a third. A death toll that changes by six figures when you use the paper's own numbers is not an estimate the study made. It is an estimate the study's numbers were made to sit still for.

The paper's own authors, to their credit, wrote the caveats into it: the gap could mean Pfizer harmed people, or that Moderna protected people better — several studies have found Moderna slightly more effective against severe COVID, which is unsurprising given it carried significantly more mRNA per dose — or that the two groups differed in health in ways matching on seven variables cannot capture. The recipients were not matched for frailty or underlying conditions. FactCheck.org's SciCheck put it to the experts. Jeffrey Morris, director of biostatistics at the University of Pennsylvania, called claims of mass death from this paper "orders of magnitude beyond what his own paper showed." Emory biostatistician Natalie Dean: "I don't see how this is an indictment of the safety of these vaccines since it's just a comparison of two different vaccines." The University of Minnesota's CIDRAP, walking through the same paper, concluded that drawing the conclusion the deaths were linked to the vaccine "is unfounded, given the observational study design." And the wider record points the other way: studies comparing vaccinated people to unvaccinated people have consistently found lower death rates in the vaccinated, including a 2025 French study following people for four years that found about a 25% lower risk of death among the vaccinated and concluded that "a causal link between mRNA vaccination and excess long-term mortality appears highly unlikely." A Veterans Affairs study in the New England Journal of Medicine that ran the same Pfizer-versus-Moderna comparison found no difference at all in non-COVID deaths — "absolutely no difference, like not even a trend," in Morris's words.

"We took a race between two horses and announced that one of them had killed half a million people," said Dale Fenn, 61, a fictional retired actuary who has been extrapolating Florida onto the entire United States since roughly the hanging chads. "The other horse is standing right there, also an mRNA vaccine, unindicted. In my profession we call that a comparison. On the internet, apparently, we call it a catastrophe, provided the multiplication is large enough."

None of this requires anyone to declare the Florida paper worthless — FactCheck.org notes the study itself was upfront about its limitations, and a real, replicated difference between the two vaccines would be worth knowing. It merely observes the distance between the paper and the post. The paper compares two vaccines in one state, attributes no deaths, and estimates no national toll. The post reports a government study estimating up to 522,000 American deaths from one vaccine alone. Between those two sentences lies one substitution of a baseline, one relabelling of a comparison as a body count, and one explainer. The study did the science. The catastrophe was added in post-production.

At press time, the unvaccinated control group the study never had could not be reached for comment on the deaths it never recorded, and the figure 522,000 had been traced to its source, which was not Florida, not the government, and not the study, but a multiplication.

Actually True

The study is real, and so is its headline statistic. Levi, Mansuri, Jordan & Ladapo, Global Epidemiology, published Nov. 4, 2026, mostly Florida Department of Health researchers using state data: 981,411 Pfizer recipients matched 1:1 to 981,411 Moderna recipients (first doses Dec. 2020–Aug. 2021), with 7,690 vs. 5,519 all-cause deaths over 12 months — a hazard ratio of 1.4 ("40% higher" relative risk), an absolute gap of 221 deaths per 100,000, and about 99% of both groups alive at one year (CIDRAP, University of Minnesota, Nov. 12, 2026; FactCheck.org SciCheck, Nov. 2026).

The study had no unvaccinated group and attributed no deaths to Pfizer. Both arms received an mRNA vaccine, so the design cannot say whether either shot raised or lowered mortality overall. The paper itself lists the possible explanations: a harmful Pfizer effect, a more protective Moderna effect (Moderna's dose carried significantly more mRNA, and several studies found it slightly more effective against severe COVID), or unmeasured differences between the groups — recipients were not matched for frailty or underlying health status (FactCheck.org; CIDRAP).

331,500 and 522,000 are Hulscher's extrapolations, not the study's estimates. 331,500 = 150 million assumed U.S. Pfizer recipients × the study's 221-per-100,000 gap versus Moderna — a between-vaccine difference, not deaths caused by Pfizer. 522,000 = 150M × an assumed 0.87% baseline mortality × 40%, substituting 0.87% for the study's actual measured Moderna mortality rate of 0.562% (Tech ARP fact-check, Nov. 2026; Hulscher's own published calculations).

Independent reviewers say causation is not established. UPenn biostatistics director Jeffrey Morris told FactCheck.org the mass-death reading is "orders of magnitude beyond what his own paper showed"; Emory's Natalie Dean said a two-vaccine comparison is not "an indictment of the safety of these vaccines"; CIDRAP concluded linking the deaths to the vaccine is "unfounded, given the observational study design."

The broader evidence runs the other way. Studies comparing vaccinated with unvaccinated people have consistently found lower all-cause mortality in the vaccinated; a 2025 French four-year study found ~25% lower death risk in the vaccinated and called a causal link between mRNA vaccination and excess long-term mortality "highly unlikely." A VA study in the New England Journal of Medicine running the same Pfizer-vs.-Moderna comparison found virtually identical non-COVID death patterns in the two groups (FactCheck.org, citing NEJM and JAMA Network research).

What is true in the original post: a government-affiliated study of an entire state's data exists, it did find a 40% higher relative 12-month mortality among Pfizer recipients than Moderna recipients, and Nicolas Hulscher is indeed the source explaining the 331,500–522,000 figures. The false parts are the attribution — the study "estimates" neither figure and attributes no deaths — and they are the parts that make the post a story.
