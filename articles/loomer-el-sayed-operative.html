Senator-Elect Is a Secret Operative, Pledge Reveals; the Pledge, Fact-Checkers Note, Was Not a Pledge

By Chip Hollister November 21, 2026 4 min read
🔎

Watch Chip Hollister tell it:

Washington, D.C. — Laura Loomer announced Saturday that Abdul El-Sayed, the physician Michigan voters just elected as the first Muslim U.S. senator, is in fact a “Muslim Brotherhood operative” who “signed a literal pledge in support of the Muslim Brotherhood.”

The literal pledge, on inspection, was a literal problem. Fact-checkers who read the 2012 statement at the center of the claim — a statement by 46 Egyptian-American community leaders backing Egypt’s elected president, Mohamed Morsi, after the ouster of Hosni Mubarak — concluded it was not a pledge of support for the Muslim Brotherhood as an organization at all. It was a statement about an elected president’s decisions, which is a different document, in the sense that it is the actual one.

“I don’t need to read the pledge to know what it pledges. The word ‘literal’ is right there in the post. That’s how you know it’s literal.”

“I don’t need to read the pledge to know what it pledges,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who investigates documents exclusively by hearing about them. “The word ‘literal’ is right there in the post. That’s how you know it’s literal.”

El-Sayed, for his part, has denied any affiliation, disavowed the group on camera at an October town hall — “Sure, yeah, absolutely,” when asked — and told Fox News, “I have never been a member of the Muslim Brotherhood, I never will be.” Investigators described the disavowal as the most operative-like move yet, in that an operative would say exactly that, and also in that anyone else would too.

The operative theory also survived El-Sayed winning the election on November 3, defeating Mike Rogers by about seven points, after PolitiFact rated the campaign version of this same claim — that he was a “jihadist Muslim Brotherhood member” — “Pants on Fire,” and rated Rogers’s claim that El-Sayed didn’t disavow the group False, because the disavowal is on tape.

At press time, the White House meeting that prompted the post had still not been explained to the investigation’s satisfaction, mostly because the investigation had not asked anyone who was at it.

Actually True

No evidence El-Sayed is a Brotherhood member or “operative,” and no pledge exists in the record. Bridge Michigan’s fact check found no evidence El-Sayed “pledged support” to the Muslim Brotherhood or has ties to it. PolitiFact investigated the campaign version of the claim — that El-Sayed was a “jihadist Muslim Brotherhood member” — and rated it “Pants on Fire,” finding no evidence to support the label or of Brotherhood membership.

The 2012 document backed an elected president, not the Brotherhood as an organization. The November 25, 2012 statement, signed by 46 Egyptian-American leaders, said they were “united in support of the elected President,” Mohamed Morsi, who was backed by the Brotherhood. PolitiFact and Bridge Michigan both concluded the statement was not a pledge of support for the Brotherhood itself.

He disavowed the group, on camera. At an October 17, 2026 town hall in Caro, Michigan, asked to disavow the Muslim Brotherhood, El-Sayed answered, “Sure, yeah, absolutely.” PolitiFact rated Mike Rogers’s claim that he “didn’t disavow” it False after reviewing the video.

The election is over, and he won it. El-Sayed defeated Rogers in the November 3, 2026 Michigan Senate race (about 52.5%, roughly seven points in unofficial results), becoming the first Muslim elected to the U.S. Senate — which makes “operative” a claim about a senator-elect, not a rumor about a candidate.

A nuance on “designated”: The U.S. has designated specific Muslim Brotherhood chapters — the Lebanese chapter as a Foreign Terrorist Organization, and the Egyptian and Jordanian chapters as Specially Designated Global Terrorists, on January 13, 2026 — not El-Sayed, and designation of foreign chapters is not evidence about a Michigan physician’s membership, for which no record has been produced.

Sources: PolitiFact fact checks on the El-Sayed Brotherhood claims (Oct 2026); Bridge Michigan fact check; U.S. Department of State / Treasury, “Terrorist Designations of Muslim Brotherhood Chapters” (Jan 13, 2026). This claim is also logged in our Lie Counter.
