Voters Asked for Lower Prices, Peace, and the Epstein Files; Congress Heard “Hold Our Beer”

By Chip Hollister November 20, 2026 3 min read
🍺

Washington, D.C. — Representative Thomas Massie announced Thursday that voters’ November 3 requests — lower prices, an end to the war, and the release of the Epstein files — had been received, processed, and replaced with a single December bill that funds the war, merges the Israeli military with America’s, spends the country into oblivion, spies on Americans, and then quits early for Christmas.

“Hold our beer,” Republicans replied, according to Massie, who posted the exchange himself, in what aides described as the most efficient legislative summary ever committed to social media.

The timing was, by Massie’s own account, impeccable. Thursday marked exactly one year since the Epstein Files Transparency Act became law, an anniversary Massie spent on the House floor promoting its sequel, the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, which would let victims, state attorneys general, and members of Congress sue the Justice Department for still withholding the files. The sequel’s discharge petition hit the 218 signatures needed to force a vote just last week — a milestone Massie celebrated by watching his colleagues prepare a bill that does, in his telling, the precise opposite of everything the voters asked for, then adjourn for the holidays.

“Voters said three things. Congress heard one thing, and that thing was beer.”

“It’s a bold strategy,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who has read the post twice. “Voters said three things. Congress heard one thing, and that thing was beer.”

At press time, the December bill had not yet been named, numbered, or read by anyone quoted in this article, which congressional staffers described as “standard procedure” and “frankly, generous — usually we don’t even get the beer part.”

Actually True

The Epstein Files Transparency Act was signed into law on November 19, 2025 — exactly one year before Massie’s post. The House passed it 427–1 on November 18, 2025 (Rep. Clay Higgins cast the lone no vote); the Senate cleared it by unanimous consent the next day, and President Trump signed it. It reached the floor only because Massie and Rep. Ro Khanna’s discharge petition collected the required 218 signatures.

Massie and Khanna introduced the follow-up, the Epstein Files Transparency Act II (H.R. 9694), on July 15, 2026. It would let state attorneys general, district attorneys, Epstein victims, and members of Congress sue the Attorney General over unlawfully withheld, redacted, delayed, or concealed Epstein records. Its discharge petition reached the 218 signatures needed to force a House vote on November 11–13, 2026.

Massie is in his final weeks in Congress: he lost his May Republican primary to Trump-endorsed Ed Gallrein and leaves office in January 2027. He has said he plans to force a vote to end the Iran war before Congress adjourns for Christmas.

What this article does not verify: Massie’s post does not name the December bill, so this article does not name one either. “Merge Israeli military with ours” and “spy on Americans” are Massie’s characterizations of it, quoted as his words — not findings about any bill text.

Sources: Epstein Files Transparency Act record (signed Nov 19, 2025; House 427–1); massie.house.gov, July 15, 2026 (EFTA II introduction, H.R. 9694); govinfo.gov — H.R. 9694; Louisville Courier Journal, Nov 18, 2026.
