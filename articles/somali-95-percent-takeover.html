Somalis Have "Completely" Taken Over Minnesota and Almost 95% Are on Welfare, Internet Announces; the Takeover, Records Show, Amounts to 2% of the State, and the 95% Appears in No Dataset Anywhere

By Biff Carrington November 22, 2026 4 min read
⚑

Minneapolis, Minn. — Minnesota has been completely taken over, the internet announced Sunday — lock, stock, and barrel — by a community that makes up about 2% of the state’s population, 95% of whom are on welfare, according to a figure that appears in no dataset, survey, or report that anyone has been able to produce.

“Trump just TORCHED the Somali Invasion of Minnesota,” wrote Benny Johnson, “and calls out that virtually all of them are on some form of welfare.” Alex Jones, arriving at the same event with a larger calculator, put the number at “Almost 95%,” and reported that the state’s new owners hold it “Lock, Stock & Barrel.”

The takeover is, by the standards of takeovers, unusually quiet. The Census Bureau’s American Community Survey counts a little over 108,000 Minnesotans of Somali descent in a state of roughly 5.8 million people — about 2% — of whom, the state demographer notes, 95% are U.S. citizens and 58% were born in the United States. A conquering force that is mostly native-born citizens is, historians confirmed, generally just called “the public.”

The 95% welfare figure has a more interesting biography. The highest number in any actual study is 89% — and it is not a share of people. It is the share of Somali immigrant households with children in which at least one member received at least one benefit, in a Center for Immigration Studies analysis of Census data. Across all Somali immigrant households the figure is 81%, and Minnesota’s state demographer, counting individuals of Somali ancestry, found 84% received assistance — mostly Medicaid, which covered 78%, while just 8% received public-assistance income. Somewhere between the household and the headline, 89 became 95, households became people, and a child on Medicaid became a dependent of the state.

“My kid’s on my insurance and I still go to work. Apparently if the insurance were Medicaid, I’d have taken over Minnesota. I have not taken over Minnesota. I can barely take over my own garage.”

“A household counts if anybody in it gets anything,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who has been counted in several households. “My kid’s on my insurance and I still go to work. Apparently if the insurance were Medicaid, I’d have taken over Minnesota. I have not taken over Minnesota. I can barely take over my own garage.”

The “supported by our tax dollars” framing also skips the employment page. When the president previously put the community’s non-working share at 92%, PolitiFact rated it Pants on Fire: Census data show 72.2% of Somali Minnesotans aged 16 and over are in the labor force and 66.6% are employed — a labor-force rate higher than Minnesotans overall. Even the CIS report’s own author explained the confusion: most of these benefits are available to workers, which is why a household can be on Medicaid and on the job at the same time.

None of this makes the underlying numbers pretty, and this newspaper is not in the business of pretending otherwise. Somali Minnesotans are poor at rates far above the state — the state demographer puts poverty at about 38% — and the fraud prosecutions in state programs are real, charged, and in the largest case already convicted. A community can be struggling, and a fraud case can be genuine, without 2% of a state owning the other 98% lock, stock, and barrel, and without a percentage being promoted past every figure its own source ever published.

At press time, Minnesota’s remaining 98% reported they had not been notified of the takeover, and planned to confirm their status the usual way: by checking whether the governor, the legislature, both senators, and the state fair were still, as of Sunday evening, exactly where they had left them.

Actually True

No dataset reaches 95%. The Center for Immigration Studies (Dec. 10, 2025), analyzing 2014–2023 American Community Survey data, found 81% of Somali immigrant households in Minnesota — and 89% of those with children — consume “some form of welfare” (cash assistance, SNAP, or Medicaid). Minnesota state demographer Susan Brower, in ACS data provided to FactCheck.org, found 84% of individuals reporting Somali ancestry received assistance income, SNAP, Medicaid, or a combination — driven by Medicaid (78% of individuals; 90% of children), with SNAP at 60% and public-assistance income at just 8% (range 6.3%–10.1%). (FactCheck.org, “Probing Trump’s Verbal Attack on Somalis,” Dec. 9, 2025, updated Dec. 17, 2025.)

Households are not people, and benefits are not unemployment. A household counts toward the 81%/89% figures if any one member receives any one benefit. PolitiFact (Feb. 2, 2026) rated the related claim that 92% of Minnesota Somalis “don’t work” Pants on Fire: 2024 ACS data show 72.2% of Somali Minnesotans 16+ in the labor force and 66.6% employed, a labor-force rate above the state’s overall ~68%. CIS author Jason Richwine told PolitiFact the high benefit figures do not mean people don’t work, because programs like SNAP and Medicaid are available to low-wage workers.

The “takeover” is about 2% of the state — and mostly American. The Census Bureau’s ACS counts more than 108,000 people of Somali descent in Minnesota (of ~260,000 nationwide), roughly 2% of the state’s population. Per the state demographer via FactCheck.org, 95% of Somalis in Minnesota are U.S. citizens and 58% were born in the U.S.

The honest context cuts the other way on poverty, not on the claim. Somali Minnesotans have genuinely high poverty (about 38%, per the state demographer) and the state’s lowest median household income ($28,500 in 2020 dollars, 2023 state report), and the Minnesota program-fraud cases are real — Feeding Our Future alone saw more than $240 million fraudulently obtained and disbursed, per the Justice Department, with its founder convicted in March 2025. Those facts support an argument about poverty and program oversight. They do not support 95%, “virtually all,” or a takeover.

Sources: FactCheck.org, “Probing Trump’s Verbal Attack on Somalis” (Dec. 9, 2025; updates Dec. 10 & 17, 2025); Center for Immigration Studies, “Somali Immigrants in Minnesota” (Dec. 2025); PolitiFact, “Trump wrong that 92% of Minnesota Somalis don’t work” (Feb. 2, 2026); U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2024.
