Starvation in Gaza Was “One Big Hoax,” Internet Announces; the Famine, the Record Shows, Was Confirmed by the IPC, Counted by WHO, and Is Still in the Books

By Chip Hollister November 24, 2026 4 min read
⚖

Watch Chip Hollister tell it:

GAZA CITY — Starvation in Gaza was “one big hoax,” Laura Loomer announced Tuesday, in a finding that will come as news to the famine classification system that confirmed a famine there, the agencies that counted the malnutrition deaths, and the 12,000 children identified as acutely malnourished in a single month.

“Turns out starvation in Gaza was one big hoax. Go figure,” Loomer wrote. “I mean let's be honest. Obesity is very common in Palestinian Arab culture. They aren't exactly missing meals.”

The hoax, on inspection, has an unusual amount of paperwork. On August 22, 2025, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification — the IPC, the multi-agency system the world's governments use to decide whether a famine is happening — confirmed famine in Gaza Governorate, the first famine ever officially confirmed in the Middle East. More than half a million people were in IPC Phase 5, Catastrophic: starvation, destitution, and preventable deaths, with the number projected to pass 640,000 within weeks. Thirty-nine percent of people surveyed said they were going days at a time without eating, and adults reported skipping meals so their children could have theirs — a meal-skipping arrangement that is difficult to square with nobody missing meals.

The children were apparently in on the hoax at scale. In July 2025 alone, more than 12,000 children were identified as acutely malnourished — the highest monthly figure ever recorded in Gaza and a six-fold increase since the start of that year. By September 5, 2025, the World Health Organization was reporting 361 Palestinians dead from malnutrition, including 130 children. Hoaxes rarely keep a death toll, and when they do, they rarely itemize it by age.

The obesity evidence, offered in rebuttal, is real and beside the point. Pre-war data put adult obesity in Palestine at 26.8% — a genuine public-health problem, measured in peacetime surveys, in a population that nutrition researchers describe as carrying a double burden of obesity and undernutrition at the same time. A population's average body weight in 2022 does not measure its food supply in 2025, any more than a full pantry in a photograph proves the house was never burgled. The famine analysts, notably, did not weigh adults and call it a day; they measured children wasting, households going days without food, and people dying of hunger, and classified accordingly.

To be fair to the record — which is more fairness than the post extends to it — the famine did end. On December 19, 2025, after the October ceasefire let aid scale up, the IPC reported there was no longer a famine in Gaza. That is how real famines behave when food arrives, and it is not how hoaxes behave at all; nobody has ever ended a hoax by delivering flour. And the improvement has limits the post does not mention: the IPC's July 2026 analysis still found over 1.2 million people — 59% of Gaza's population — in Crisis-level food insecurity or worse, including 212,000 people in Emergency, with about 1.4 million projected in Crisis or worse through December 2026.

“It was a hoax, and also it stopped when the food showed up, and also a million people are still in a food crisis,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who follows famines the way other men follow football. “That's a hoax with a relief operation, a ceasefire effect, and a two-year statistical tail. Most hoaxes I know are just a guy in a costume.”

At press time, the hoax remained officially confirmed in the historical record by the FAO, UNICEF, the World Food Programme, and the World Health Organization, and the people who were not missing meals were still, in the IPC's most recent count, 1.2 million people in a food crisis — missing, by the classification's definition, rather a lot of them.

Actually True

Famine was formally confirmed in Gaza — it was not a hoax. On August 22, 2025, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) confirmed famine (Phase 5) in Gaza Governorate — the first famine officially confirmed in the Middle East. More than 500,000 people faced starvation, destitution, and preventable deaths, projected to rise past 640,000 by the end of September 2025. Famine classification requires all three thresholds — extreme food deprivation, acute malnutrition, and starvation-related deaths — to be breached.

People were going days without eating. In the IPC's July 2025 data, 39% of people reported going days at a time without eating, households reporting very severe hunger had doubled since May (and more than tripled in Gaza City), and adults regularly skipped meals to feed their children.

Children were malnourished at record levels. In July 2025 alone, more than 12,000 children were identified as acutely malnourished — the highest monthly figure ever recorded, a six-fold increase since the start of 2025 — and the number of children expected to be at severe risk of death from malnutrition tripled to 43,400.

There is a counted death toll. WHO's September 2025 public-health analysis reported 361 Palestinians had died due to malnutrition as of September 5, 2025, including 130 children.

The famine ending does not make it a hoax — and the crisis is not over. The IPC reported on December 19, 2025 that famine was no longer present after the October 2025 ceasefire allowed aid to scale up. Its July 23, 2026 analysis still found over 1.2 million people (59% of the population) in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse, including 212,000 in Phase 4 (Emergency), with 1.4 million (67%) projected in Crisis or worse through December 2026.

Pre-war obesity does not rebut a wartime famine. Adult obesity in Palestine was measured at 26.8% in pre-war data (WHO figures reviewed in Frontiers in Nutrition, 2023) — a real double-burden problem in which obesity and undernutrition coexist in the same population. A peacetime obesity rate says nothing about food access during the 2025 famine, which was measured directly through child wasting, household food consumption, and hunger deaths.
