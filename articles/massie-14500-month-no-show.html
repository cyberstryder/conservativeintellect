House Adjourns Early for Lack of Members, Then Begs Members to Show Up

By Chip Hollister November 21, 2026 3 min read
🏛

Washington, D.C. — The House of Representatives adjourned Thursday until November 30, having canceled Friday’s votes for lack of members in town, and on Friday morning Speaker Mike Johnson issued a formal letter begging the missing members to please come back to work.

“We must have full attendance and the support of our entire conference,” Johnson wrote, in a letter that did not address the question of whose job it is to have the conference in town, or what the conference was doing instead. “This week we had to postpone the rule scheduled for the Floor because we didn’t have enough Members in town.”

Representative Thomas Massie, who was in town, noticed. “The Speaker had to adjourn session early because so many Republicans refused to show up for work that we didn’t have a majority,” Massie posted Friday. “I get that members are demoralized from losing elections… but they still collect $14,500/month!”

“First you send everyone home early, then you write them a letter asking them to come back. It’s like quitting your job and then emailing your boss to ask if there’s any work.”

The Speaker’s letter counts down 44 days until the Republican majority ends on January 3, a deadline the House will meet with three work weeks after Thanksgiving, most of them without votes scheduled, and government funding expiring December 11 — a schedule aides described as “ambitious” and “definitely a schedule.”

“It’s a bold management strategy,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who has read the letter twice. “First you send everyone home early, then you write them a letter asking them to come back. It’s like quitting your job and then emailing your boss to ask if there’s any work.”

At press time, the House was still adjourned, the members were still collecting $14,500 a month, and the rule was still postponed, pending the return of the town’s missing members.

Actually True

The House did adjourn early: leaders canceled Friday’s votes and the chamber adjourned Thursday until November 30. Speaker Johnson’s own letter, posted Friday on X, confirms the cause: “This week we had to postpone the rule scheduled for the Floor because we didn’t have enough Members in town.”

The absences are counted, not rumored: Johnson told reporters Thursday there were 14 absences the day before. Veteran congressional reporter Jamie Dupree counted nine Republicans and three Democrats who missed every House vote this week, leaving Republicans without a floor majority.

$14,500 a month is exact: members of Congress are paid $174,000 a year — $14,500 a month — a rate frozen since 2009 (Congressional Research Service). The Speaker makes $223,500.

The clock Johnson is counting down is real: 44 days until the Republican majority ends January 3; Congress has three work weeks left after Thanksgiving, and government funding expires December 11.

What this article does not verify: former Rep. Greene’s “32 members” figure — the verified counts are 14 absent on one day and 12 missing every vote all week; her 32 does not match either published count. And “scared of Massie’s forced Epstein vote” is her characterization of Johnson’s motive, not a finding. What is procedurally real is the vote itself — Massie and Khanna’s Epstein Files Transparency Act II discharge petition reached the 218 signatures needed to force a vote on November 11–13, 2026.
