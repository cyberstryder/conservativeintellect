Congresswoman "Isn't Even a Legitimate U.S. Citizen," Commentator Announces; She Became a Citizen in 2000, Records Show, and Has Been One for 25 Years — 19 of Them Before Her First Day in the House, Which Requires Seven

By Chip Hollister November 25, 2026 4 min read
🛂

WASHINGTON — The real Constitutional crisis, a commentator announced Wednesday, is "finding out someone who isn't even a legitimate U.S. citizen has been serving in Congress" — the someone being Rep. Ilhan Omar, who became a U.S. citizen in the year 2000 and has the paperwork-shaped biography to prove it.

"Flashback to Ilhan Omar melting down that shutting down USAID was a 'Constitutional crisis,'" wrote Benny Johnson. "The real Constitutional crisis is finding out someone who isn't even a legitimate U.S. citizen has been serving in Congress."

Here is what there is to find out. Omar was born in Mogadishu in 1982, fled Somalia's civil war at eight, spent four years in a refugee camp in Kenya, and arrived in the United States in 1995, when the United States granted her family asylum. In 2000, at 17, she became a U.S. citizen, acquiring citizenship through her father's naturalization, as minor children do. This is not a contested timeline unearthed by investigators; it is the second paragraph of her Britannica entry, and it has been sitting in public, in roughly that wording, for years.

The Constitution, whose crisis this is said to be, asks comparatively little here. Article I requires a member of the House to have been a citizen of the United States for seven years. Omar had been a citizen for nineteen years when Minnesota's Fifth District first sent her to Congress in 2018, and she has since been elected, certified, and sworn in repeatedly, each time as what she is: a naturalized citizen, which is a kind of legitimate U.S. citizen — in fact, under the Fourteenth Amendment and federal law, the only distinction between her citizenship and a birthright citizen's is that hers can be revoked if the government proves in court that it was obtained by fraud. No court has found that. No denaturalization judgment against her exists.

"We checked her legitimacy the way we check everything: by reading the announcement," said Dale Krebbs, 54, a fictional local man who keeps his own citizenship in a drawer, where it remains legitimate because nobody has posted otherwise. "Turns out a congresswoman's status is decided by courts and certificates, not by finding out. Finding out is just reading. I do it every morning and it has never revoked anything yet."

To be fair to the record — this newspaper insists on it, especially when it is inconvenient — there is a real investigation. In October 2026, Justice Department agents searched Omar's Washington-area home as part of a probe into her family's immigration documentation, including, per the Wall Street Journal's reporting, whether her age was misstated on citizenship paperwork decades ago. Investigators seized her passport and devices; subpoenas went to relatives. That is a serious matter, and this newspaper covered its unsealed filings on November 24: they contained no charges, the suspected offenses are redacted, and the government's own lawyers described a pre-indictment investigation. An allegation being investigated is the beginning of a legal process. It is not its conclusion, and it does not travel backward in time to make a quarter-century of documented citizenship illegitimate in the meantime. If a court ever finds fraud, the finding will be news, and this newspaper will print it in the same size type as today's announcement — which is to say, the size type reserved for things that have actually happened.

None of this requires anyone to like Omar's politics, her votes, or her view of USAID, all of which remain available for ordinary criticism in unlimited quantities. It merely observes the distance between the record and the summary. The record says: refugee in 1995, citizen in 2000, congresswoman since 2019, investigated in 2026, charged with nothing in the unsealed filings. The summary says: not a legitimate citizen, full stop, crisis declared. One of these is a biography. The other is a verdict issued by a post.

At press time, the year 2000 had been located in Omar's biography, where it has been all along, the seven-year constitutional clock had been checked and found to have run out sometime in 2007, and the crisis had been returned to the Constitution, which reported that it was fine and had, in fact, been the document that naturalized her in the first place — process-wise.

Actually True

Omar became a U.S. citizen in 2000, at age 17. Encyclopaedia Britannica's biography: her family fled Somalia, lived four years in a Kenyan refugee camp, was granted asylum in the United States in 1995, and "Omar became a U.S. citizen at the age of 17 in 2000." The Biographical Directory of the United States Congress and her House materials carry the same timeline; she acquired citizenship through her father's naturalization.

She easily meets the Constitution's citizenship requirement for the House. Article I requires seven years of citizenship for a representative. Citizen since 2000, first sworn in January 2019 — nineteen years a citizen on day one, twenty-five years today. She has been elected and certified in Minnesota's Fifth District in every cycle since 2018.

Naturalized citizenship is full citizenship, revocable only on a court finding of fraud. A naturalized citizen can be denaturalized only if the government proves in federal court that citizenship was procured by concealment of a material fact or willful misrepresentation. No court has made any such finding against Omar, and no denaturalization judgment against her exists; earlier official reviews of fraud tips (FBI, 2019–20; House Ethics Committee) closed without criminal charges.

A real investigation exists — and it is an allegation, not a finding. In October 2026 the Justice Department searched Omar's home in a probe of her family's immigration documentation, reportedly including whether her age was misstated on decades-old paperwork (Wall Street Journal, via subsequent reporting). The unsealed filings contained no charges, the suspected offenses are redacted, and prosecutors described a pre-indictment investigation (see this site's November 24, 2026 article, "omar-investigation-1980").

What is true in the original post: Omar did criticize the USAID shutdown in constitutional terms, she was born in Somalia and is a naturalized — not birthright — citizen, and her citizenship is currently the subject of a federal investigation. "Isn't even a legitimate U.S. citizen" is the false part: as of today she is one, and has been for 25 years.
