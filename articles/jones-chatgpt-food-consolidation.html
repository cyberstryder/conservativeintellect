ChatGPT Confirms Government Is Taking All the Emergency Food, Reports Broadcaster

By Chip Hollister November 20, 2026 4 min read
⚑

Austin, Texas — Alex Jones announced Friday that the federal government has begun the “National Forced Consolidation Of Emergency Food Stocks,” that America’s top storable food companies have been told to “CEASE SALES TO THE AMERICAN PEOPLE,” and that the whole thing has been confirmed by the highest authority available on short notice: ChatGPT.

“ALEX JONES FIRST BROKE The News,” Jones added, in a follow-up line officials described as the best-sourced sentence in the announcement, in that it could be verified against the post directly above it.

The Pentagon’s actual program, FORTRESS, was announced on September 30 — roughly seven weeks ago, not three — and it is a plan to let major military bases keep running for 90 days if they are cut off from the civilian grid. It is, in other words, a plan for the government to feed its own bases, which Jones helpfully translated as a plan for the government to take everyone else’s food, sell none of it back, and confirm the arrangement through a chatbot.

“This man got a chatbot to agree with him and called it confirmed. I asked my GPS if I was right about something yesterday and it just rerouted me.”

The confirmation process impressed preparedness experts. “Normally you need a document, an order, a named official, something,” said Dale Krebbs, 54, a local man who has read the post twice. “This man got a chatbot to agree with him and called it confirmed. I asked my GPS if I was right about something yesterday and it just rerouted me.”

Neither DHS nor FEMA has announced any such order. No storable food company has announced it was ordered to stop selling to the public. The only published version of the cease-sales order is on Jones’s own outlet, which reported Friday that “Alex Jones personally broke the news” — a sourcing chain experts described as “circular, but at least it’s a complete circle.”

The administration Jones says is quietly seizing the food supply spent the previous week saying the opposite on television. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said on November 15 that he is not stockpiling and that the panic over 90-day outages is “unfounded.” Vice President JD Vance said on November 16 that friends asking whether to stock up on food and water should not, because “we don’t see a crisis coming down.”

At press time, FEMA’s public guidance still asked households to keep several days of food and water — the same several days it has asked for all along — and the emergency food remained, by all available evidence, for sale to the American people, including, presumably, to broadcasters.

Actually True

FORTRESS is real — and it is about bases, not households. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth unveiled FORTRESS (Federal Operations for Resilience, Troop Resources, Energy, Supply, and Survivability) on September 30, 2026 — about seven weeks before Jones’s post, not “three weeks ago.” Its reported goal is that major U.S. military bases operate independently for at least 90 days with their own electricity, water, fuel, and communications if cut off from civilian infrastructure. It contains no order to civilians and no seizure of the public food supply.

No cease-sales order has been announced by anyone but Jones. The only published account of DHS/FEMA ordering national storable food companies to stop sales to the public is a November 20 item on Jones’s own outlet, which says Jones “personally broke the news.” No DHS or FEMA announcement, no food company statement, and no reputable news outlet reporting such an order was found in this check.

FEMA’s household guidance is unchanged — and it is days, not months. Ready.gov’s Build A Kit page still tells households to keep food, water, and supplies “to last for several days”: one gallon of water per person per day for several days, and at least a several-day supply of non-perishable food. There is no 90-day household directive and no shortage warning attached to it.

The administration said the opposite, on the record, days earlier. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin told Fox News on November 15: “I’m not stockpiling. We are in good shape here. The panic that people want to draw from is unfounded.” Vice President JD Vance said on November 16 that friends asked whether to build a bunker or stock up on food and water — “My answer to that is no. We don’t see a crisis coming down” — and called the base-preparation reporting “basic good government.”

ChatGPT is not a confirming authority. Jones’s post names no DHS/FEMA document, order number, official, or food company. Its named confirmer is a chatbot. A chatbot agreeing with a claim is not a government announcement, and no underlying order was produced with the post.

Sources: The Despoiler, Oct 29, 2026; Ready.gov — Build A Kit; CNN, Nov 17, 2026. This claim is also logged in our Lie Counter.
