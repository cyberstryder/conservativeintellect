School Board Votes to Replace History Curriculum with VibesSample

By Tad Pemberton September 21, 2026 4 min read
§

Mesa, Arizona — In a unanimous 5–0 vote Tuesday night, the Mesa Unified School Board eliminated the district’s history curriculum, replacing it with what officials described as “a robust, standards-aligned program of vibes.”

“Students were learning things that made them feel bad, which is the opposite of learning,” explained board president Karen Dulles, 61, who has not read a book since 1987 but “heard about several and didn’t care for the tone.” “Under the new curriculum, if a historical event gives you a bad vibe, it simply didn’t happen. That’s just science.”

The new framework, titled America: Pretty Great, Probably, covers the nation’s past in a single 45-minute unit. Key topics include: the founding (good vibes), the flag (great vibes), and “everything else” (complicated vibes, skipped).

“We asked the students what they thought of history, and 9 out of 10 said it was ‘mid.’ We can’t argue with data.”

Tests will no longer be graded on correctness. Instead, students will be evaluated on confidence, patriotism, and “general aura.” A leaked rubric shows that answering “freedom” to any question earns partial credit, while answering “let me think about that” is an automatic F.

Teachers have been instructed to replace textbooks with a single laminated card reading “IT ALL WORKED OUT.” The district saved $1.2 million on books, which it immediately spent on a 40-foot bronze eagle for the administration building parking lot.

Local historian Dr. Samuel Okafor called the decision “the single most efficient destruction of knowledge I’ve ever witnessed,” adding, “It took other regimes years of book-burning to achieve what this board did in one meeting with a PowerPoint.”

Board president Dulles dismissed the criticism. “Historians are just people who memorized the past instead of moving on from it,” she said. “Very low-vibe profession.”

At press time, the board was voting on whether math “feels kind of judgy” and should also be replaced. Early polling suggests numbers are “on thin ice.”
